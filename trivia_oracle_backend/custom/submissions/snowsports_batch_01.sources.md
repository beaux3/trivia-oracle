# Sources for snowsports_batch_01

Verification standard: the requester asked for **one confirming source per fact**. Every URL below was
opened during research, except the two marked "search snippet". Facts that could not be confirmed were left out.

## 1. bindings (difficulty 2)
- Saf-Ski 1937 toe release, Teflon anti-friction pad around 1972, toe and heel pieces, brake, DIN settings by weight, height and ability: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_binding
- DIN scale (search snippet only): https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/din-setting-for-ski-bindings/

## 2. Les Trois Vallées (4)
- 183 lifts (3 cable cars, 37 gondolas, 69 chairlifts), largest fully connected ski area, ~600 km, interconnected 1971, La Tania for 1992 Olympics, Orelle joined 1995-97 and highest point 3,230 m, Savoie, Tarentaise, Vanoise National Park, resort list: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Trois_Vall%C3%A9es

## 3. La Plagne (4)
- Eleven villages, Montchavin 1,250 m, Glacier de la Chiaupe 3,250 m, opened 24 Dec 1961 with two drag lifts and four slopes, Aime-La Plagne 1969, 1992 bobsleigh track reused in 2030, 225 km, 2014 most popular resort (2.5 million visitors), Vanoise Express and Paradiski link 2003: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Plagne
- "Savoie" is not stated on the page as fetched (general knowledge).

## 4. Burton (3)
- BB1 single strap with rope and handle, Snurfer 1965, founded 1977 Londonderry VT, Manchester 1978, Burlington, Suicide Six 1982, U.S. Open 1985, 40-45% market share: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Snowboards
- "Named for the middle name of its founder" (Jake Burton Carpenter): the page names the founder in full; the middle-name wording is the same fact.

## 5. catching an edge (2)
- Downhill edge digs in, sideways velocity, toe or heel edge, beginners' painful reputation, novices do it many times before carving: search results quoting https://www.mechanicsofsport.com/snowboarding/catching_an_edge.html (search snippet, page not opened). "Steel rim" is the question's wording for the metal edge.

## 6. UCPA (5)
- Founded 1965 by Maurice Herzog, non-profit of government bodies, sports federations and youth associations, Paris, ~50 countries, ~250,000 travellers a year, ages 18-45 (up to 55 advanced), three activities incl. vocational training: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCPA
- Herzog led the 1950 Annapurna expedition: general knowledge, not re-checked on a page here.

## 7. button lift (3)
- Disc between the legs, snowboarders hold it with hands, low capacity, 3.0 m/s vs about 4 m/s detachable, "Poma lift", no grip strength vs rope tow, disc vs T-bar: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_lift
- The clue about the manufacturer being French is general knowledge (Poma).

## 8. gondola (2)
- Kohlerer Bahn, Bolzano, 1908; spring-loaded grips; mono/bi/tricable, bicable more stable in wind; Metrocable and Mi Teleférico: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondola_lift
- The link to the Venetian boat is general knowledge.

## 9. après-ski (2)
- French for "after skiing", ski from Old Norse via Norwegian, name chosen for popularity in French Alps, last run of the day in ski gear, most popular in Alps and spread worldwide: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apr%C3%A8s-ski

## 10. salopettes (3)
- Overalls from the 1890s via Levi Strauss & Co., Depression-era workers, denim/corduroy/chino/leather, salopettes as wind- and waterproof adapted versions with adjustable braces for sailing, skiing, diving, dungarees in British English: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overalls

## 11. wax (2)
- Applied to skis/snowboards/toboggans to adjust friction; glide wax vs. grip/kick wax; klister for old/wet snow, hard wax for fresh snow; paraffin, fluorocarbons, graphite; repairs base scratches and gouges: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_wax

## 12. Salomon (3)
- Founded 1947, Annecy, one-room metalworking shop, François Salomon, son Georges, steel ski edges: https://www.flatspot.com/blogs/blog/the-history-of-salomon
- 1957 Skade toe piece, leading binding maker by 1972, 1979 SX90 boot, 1980 SNS Nordic system: https://www.mastershoe.co.uk/salomon-history
- Adidas bought Salomon Group 1997, sold to Amer Sports 2005: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomon_Group

## 13. Superski / Dolomiti Superski (4)
- Created 1974, ~3,000 km² of northeastern Italy: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomiti_Superski
- 12 resorts, 450 lifts, 1,246 km of slopes on one pass, largest ski area in the world, ~97% snowmaking coverage: https://www.valgardena.it/en/winter-holidays-dolomites/ski-resort-val-gardena/val-gardena/dolomiti-superski/

## 14. La Folie Douce (3)
- Founded 1974 in Val d'Isère by Luc Reversade (ex-ski instructor) with his mother and chef Mamone, inspired by Ibiza/Turkey/Miami party spots: https://magazine.lecollectionist.com/en/discover-la-folie-douce-val-dis%C3%A8re
- 1980 open-air bar, live music and performers, expansion to Val Thorens, Méribel, Les Arcs, Alpe d'Huez, Val d'Isère site at 2,400 m: https://www.lafoliedouce.com/en/val-isere.html

## 15. Geneva (2)
- Airport established 1919, terminal 1946, bi-national layout on the Swiss-French border, ~17.8 million passengers in 2024: https://www.alps2alps.com/wiki/airports/geneva/
- Busiest gateway to Portes du Soleil, Mont Blanc massif, Tarentaise Valley; closest major airport to Chamonix, Verbier, Morzine: https://www.alps2alps.com/blog/geneva-airport-gateway-alps

## 16. Mont Blanc (2)
- Official height 4,807 m (2021 measurement), on the French-Italian border, south of Chamonix, "Roof of Europe": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Blanc
- First ascent 8 August 1786 by Michel-Gabriel Paccard and Jacques Balmat, called the birth of mountaineering: https://1786.travel/en/blog/august-8-1786-the-story-of-the-first-ascent-of-mont-blanc

## 17. Japow (3)
- Portmanteau of "Japan" and "powder"; cold Siberian air, Sea of Japan moisture, orographic lift over Japanese mountains: https://www.japowdb.com/en/blog/what-is-japow/
- "Japanuary" for peak conditions, especially prized in Hokkaido: https://www.tohokukanko.jp/en/features/detail_205.html

## 18. Niseko United (4)
- Four resorts (Grand Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village, Annupuri) on one mountain in Hokkaido: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niseko_United
- Niseko Free Passport launched Nov 1993, extended to an All Mountain Pass covering all lifts by 1998: https://japanskiexperience.com/news-and-articles/the-history-of-niseko-united-how-four-resorts-became-one/
- Tokyu Land Corp controlling Grand Hirafu since 2004, Hong Kong firm (PCPD) running Hanazono since 2007: same source

## 19. Hakuba Valley (3)
- Happo-One hosted 1998 Winter Olympics downhill, super-G, combined and ski jumping: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakuba_Happoone_Winter_Resort
- 10 resorts, 92 lifts, 137 runs over ~30 km, 11+ m of annual snowfall: https://www.snowmonkeyresorts.com/activities/discover-the-spirit-of-the-1998-olympics/

## 20. Rhythm / Rythm (5)
- Founded 2005 by Australians Matthew Hampton and Mick Klima, seven stores across Niseko, Hakuba, Furano: https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/australian-owned-rhythm-japan-acquired-by-leading-north-american-snowsports-retailer.html
- Acquired by evo in 2022, Hampton became evo's APAC managing director: same source
